# Scoping Review of AI, Metrology, and ESG in the Semiconductor Sector: Implications for Safe and Sustainable by Design (SSbD) - Summary

## Source
- Original file: [[brain/raw/_ID-286_Current_Version.md]]
- Ingested: 2026-06-10

## Core Content
This scoping review analyzes the "Twin Transition" in the semiconductor industry, characterized by the simultaneous push for AI-driven manufacturing scalability and compliance with stringent ESG mandates such as the [[concepts/sustainability-standards|Sustainability Standards]]. Based on a [[concepts/bibliometric-analysis|bibliometric analysis]] of 1,465 documents, the authors identify a fragmented research landscape containing significant [[concepts/structural-hole|structural holes]] between AI process optimization and sustainability governance. 

To bridge these gaps, the study proposes a 6-layer [[concepts/system-of-systems|System of Systems (SoS)]] architecture for [[concepts/safe-and-sustainable-by-design|Safe and Sustainable by Design (SSbD)]] manufacturing. This framework leverages [[concepts/virtual-metrology|Virtual Metrology]], [[concepts/machine-learning|Machine Learning]], and [[concepts/regtech|RegTech]] to integrate material safety and environmental data into production workflows. By proposing the [[concepts/grid-to-core-pathway|Grid-to-Core Pathway]] and the [[concepts/standards-through-supply-chain-pathway|Standards-Through-Supply-Chain Pathway]], the authors demonstrate how regulatory compliance can transition from a downstream audit burden into a proactive driver for innovation and circular value chains.

## Main Points
- The semiconductor sector is currently navigating a high-stakes "Twin Transition" involving both digital process scaling and mandatory climate-neutrality compliance.
- Bibliometric analysis highlights a critical disconnect between AI-driven technical process engineering and corporate sustainability governance.
- A proposed 6-layer architecture provides a roadmap to unify industrial metrology, supply chain ESG, and regulatory reporting into a coherent, provenance-aware data fabric.
- Regulatory frameworks like [[entities/corporate-supply-chain-due-diligence-directive|corporate-supply-chain-due-diligence-directive]] and CBAM are positioned as potential catalysts for systemic innovation rather than mere compliance requirements.
- The use of [[concepts/machine-learning|Machine Learning]] and sovereign data spaces is essential to facilitate secure, multi-stakeholder collaboration without compromising proprietary process data.
