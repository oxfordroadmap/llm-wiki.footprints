# The Global Business Services maturity model - Summary

## Source
- Original file: [[brain/raw/KPMG_2016-GBS.md]]
- Ingested: 2026-06-11

## Core Content
This document outlines the five-stage [[concepts/business-services-maturity-model|Business Services Maturity Model]] developed by [[entities/kpmg|KPMG]]. The model defines [[concepts/global-business-services|Global Business Services]] (GBS) as a next-generation operational framework that integrates back-, middle-, and front-office functions. Based on the [[concepts/gbs-maturity-research-study|GBS Maturity Research Study]], which analyzed over 200 companies, the report serves as a strategic roadmap for enterprises to evolve from fragmented, decentralized operations to highly integrated, outcome-oriented delivery. Organizations that successfully progress through these maturity levels can achieve significant operational cost reductions and improved business agility. 

The framework is supported by 10 [[concepts/operational-dimensions|Operational Dimensions]], ranging from talent management to [[concepts/end-to-end-process-optimization|End-to-end Process Optimization]]. While the model provides a path for transformation, it cautions against the universal pursuit of the highest maturity level, emphasizing that organizations must align their GBS journey with specific business strategies, investment capabilities, and cultural readiness to avoid excessive complexity.

## Main Points
- GBS is a multi-level operational model that integrates diverse back-, middle-, and front-office functions.
- The maturity model consists of five distinct stages: Fragmented, Sub-scaled, Scaled, Integrated, and Strategic.
- Progression up the maturity curve correlates with increased cost savings—up to 40 percent—and enhanced financial performance.
- Optimization is enabled by 10 operational dimensions, including governance, talent, and data analytics.
- Higher maturity is not always optimal; organizations should calibrate their GBS strategy based on specific enterprise needs rather than pursuing maturity for its own sake.
