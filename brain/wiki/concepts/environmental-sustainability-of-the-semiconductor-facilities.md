# environmental-sustainability-of-the-semiconductor-facilities

### Definition
Environmental sustainability of semiconductor facilities refers to the integration of green manufacturing practices, resource efficiency, and carbon footprint reduction strategies within semiconductor fabrication plants (fabs). It encompasses the systematic management of energy consumption, chemical usage, water recycling, and waste output to align semiconductor production with global ecological standards and corporate social responsibility goals.

### Key Characteristics
*   **Energy Efficiency**: Optimizing fabrication processes and facility infrastructure to minimize electricity consumption, often involving the transition to renewable energy sources.
*   **Resource Management**: Implementing closed-loop water systems and advanced chemical handling to reduce the extraction of natural resources and mitigate hazardous emissions.
*   **Lifecycle Assessment**: Evaluating the environmental impact of device production from raw material sourcing to the end-of-life stage, as monitored by frameworks like the [[entities/ieee-international-roadmap-for-devices-and-systems|IEEE IRDS]].
*   **Decarbonization**: Strategic efforts to reduce Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas emissions in line with science-based climate targets.

### Applications
*   **Green Fab Design**: Constructing new facilities with LEED-certified standards and energy-efficient vacuum and lithography systems.
*   **Supply Chain Optimization**: Ensuring that downstream suppliers adhere to environmental standards, as seen in programs like [[entities/supply-online-360|Supply Online 360]].
*   **Reporting and Compliance**: Aligning facility operations with international benchmarks to meet the criteria set by organizations like the [[entities/science-based-targets-initiative|Science Based Targets initiative]].
