# Negative feedback loops

## Definition
A systems-theory mechanism that counteracts system deviations to maintain stability. In industrial stacks, these loops are used to govern environmental impacts by exposing upstream physical costs—often externalized—to downstream accountability mechanisms, thereby ensuring resource usage and emissions are governed by data-sharing architectures.

## Key Characteristics
- **Stabilization**: Functions to dampen fluctuations and return system processes to an equilibrium or target state.
- **Circular Integration**: Transforms linear technology stacks into circular models by internalizing environmental externalities.
- **Data-Driven Accountability**: Leverages data-sharing architectures to ensure that operational outputs are measured against resource and emission inputs.
- **Upstream-Downstream Alignment**: Bridges the gap between initial resource extraction/usage and final economic output accountability.

## Applications
- **Industrial Stack Regulation**: Monitoring environmental footprints of semiconductor manufacturing via automated control loops.
- **Circular Economy Implementation**: Linking upstream environmental data to downstream supply chain accountability mechanisms.
- **SSbD Governance**: Utilizing feedback loops to enforce "Safe and Sustainable by Design" criteria across complex value chains.
