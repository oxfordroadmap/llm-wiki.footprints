# Five-layer industrial stack

## Definition
The five-layer industrial stack is a theoretical framework describing the interdependent infrastructure of modern Artificial Intelligence, consisting of energy, chips, infrastructure, models, and applications. It illustrates how these layers form a vertically integrated dependency chain that drives exponential growth in computational demand.

## Key Characteristics
- **Interdependency**: Each layer relies on the technical and physical output of the preceding layer.
- **Physical Precursors**: Highlights the reliance of digital AI models on upstream environmental and physical inputs, such as energy generation and semiconductor fabrication.
- **Economic Momentum**: Represents the rapid expansion of AI as a cohesive industrial engine.
- **Accountability Gap**: Emphasizes the disconnect between downstream digital token generation and the environmental footprint produced at the energy and chip-manufacturing levels.

## Applications
- **Policy Making**: Used to frame circular governance mechanisms that connect AI application performance to environmental accountability.
- **Technology Roadmapping**: Serves as a guide for stakeholders to identify resource bottlenecks within the AI value chain.
- **Sustainability Assessment**: Used to bridge the gap between digital resource consumption and material impact, supporting initiatives like [[concepts/safe-and-sustainable-by-design|Safe and Sustainable by Design]].
