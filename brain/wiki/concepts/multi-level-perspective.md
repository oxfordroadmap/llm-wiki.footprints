# Multi-Level Perspective

## Definition
The Multi-Level Perspective (MLP) is a socio-technical theory that analyzes transitions across three nested levels: micro-level niches, meso-level regimes, and macro-level landscapes. It provides a framework for understanding how radical innovations emerge, stabilize, and eventually transform or replace existing systems.

## Key Characteristics
- **Nested Structure**: Operates across three distinct analytical levels (niche, regime, landscape).
- **Socio-technical focus**: Recognizes that technological change is inseparable from social, cultural, and political contexts.
- **Dynamic Interaction**: Explains how macro-level pressures (landscape) create windows of opportunity for innovations (niches) to destabilize incumbent practices (regimes).
- **Operational Alignment**: Serves as a mechanism to analyze how organizational units, such as GBS units, function as "operational airlocks" to translate external pressures into strategic internal transitions.

## Applications
- Analyzing the progression of the [[concepts/twin-transition|Twin Transition]] within multinational corporations.
- Predicting technological trends that may destabilize existing industry regimes.
- Understanding the diffusion of innovations from protected niche environments into mainstream market applications.
- Evaluating the impact of policy and external environmental pressures on organizational infrastructure.
