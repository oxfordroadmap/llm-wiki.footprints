## Description
CBAM functions as a critical enforcement mechanism within the EU regulatory landscape, contrasting with voluntary frameworks like the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) by shifting the focus from ambition to direct accountability. The regulation demands granular, location-based emission values for imported goods, which are verified through customs procedures and subject to potential trade penalties. By anchoring these results into immutable structures like Digital Product Passports, CBAM facilitates automated customs clearance and carbon-tariff reconciliations. Furthermore, the mechanism serves as a catalyst for industrial data integrity, requiring supply chain transparency that bridges the gap between manufacturing output and regulatory compliance. Ultimately, it impacts the socio-economic stability of global trade by internalizing carbon costs for carbon-intensive products entering the European market.
