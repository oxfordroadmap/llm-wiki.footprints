## Definition
Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) is a systematic framework used to evaluate, rank, and prioritize competing objectives or trade-offs within complex systems. It provides a structured approach for integrating diverse, often conflicting variables—such as environmental sustainability, operational yield, and financial constraints—to support informed decision-making.

## Key Characteristics
- **Multi-Objective Synthesis:** Balances disparate goals that cannot be reduced to a single unit of measurement.
- **Trade-off Management:** Explicitly addresses conflicts between variables to identify optimal compromise solutions.
- **Decision Support:** Provides a mathematical or qualitative framework to facilitate complex choices under uncertainty.
- **System Stability:** Acts as a convergence plane to maintain dynamic equilibrium in socio-technical environments.

## Applications
- **Smart Manufacturing:** Balancing production throughput against energy efficiency and carbon impact in smart fab environments.
- **Policy Formulation:** Evaluating the success of socio-technical roadmaps by weighing stakeholder requirements against regulatory and environmental constraints.
- **Resource Management:** Optimizing the distribution of limited resources across complex system-of-systems architectures.
