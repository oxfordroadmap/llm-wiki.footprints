# Labor Arbitrage

## Definition
Labor arbitrage is a strategic practice involving the relocation of business processes to geographic regions or operational environments where labor costs are significantly lower, aimed at achieving cost efficiency and scale.

## Key Characteristics
- Focuses on the exploitation of wage differentials between global locations.
- Typically serves as a primary driver for the initial stages of Global Business Services maturity (Levels 1 and 2).
- Primarily targets cost reduction and operational efficiency.
- Frequently acts as a transitional strategy during the shift from decentralized functional structures to centralized shared service models.

## Applications
- Establishing offshore or nearshore shared service centers.
- Outsourcing non-core business processes to regions with lower prevailing wage structures.
- Initial scaling of service delivery models within Global Business Services organizations.
